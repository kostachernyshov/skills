# Persona: Dr. Julia Brandt

Head of the molecular pathology lab at a regional cancer center. Pathologist. She runs EGFR tests for lung cancer.

## What she says first
"Our hospital board wants to cut costs. Their idea: do not send EGFR tests for lung adenocarcinoma patients who are heavy smokers, because EGFR mutations are a 'never-smoker disease'. I must say yes or no at the next board meeting. Use TCGA lung adenocarcinoma data: the clinical table and the mutation calls."

## Answers she gives when asked
- Decision: keep EGFR testing for all adenocarcinoma patients, or skip it for heavy smokers. She recommends; the hospital board decides.
- Reader: the hospital board (managers, one oncologist). No statistics.
- Cost of error: a missed EGFR-sensitizing mutation means a patient does not get an EGFR inhibitor, which is the best first-line treatment for these patients. This is much worse than the cost of one test.
- Which mutations matter: those that make a patient eligible for an EGFR inhibitor. She does not know which TCGA calls count; she asks for a recommendation.
- "Heavy smoker": she does not have a definition; she asks for a recommendation.
- Acceptable miss rate: she does not know. She asks for a recommendation.
- Population: patients with lung adenocarcinoma at her center (mostly European ancestry).
- Deadline: one week. Format: one page and a few slides for the board.

She does not know anything about the data files.
