# Slide criteria

| Criterion | 2 (good) | 0 (fails) |
|---|---|---|
| message | The title is one full sentence that states what the slide shows. A reader who reads only the titles gets the story. | The title is a topic ("Results", "Survival analysis"). |
| one-idea | The slide has one idea. | Two or more ideas compete. |
| load | 5 bullets or fewer, 15 words or fewer each; one figure or one table. Space is left around content. | Text fills the slide, or a figure and a table and many bullets share it. |
| figure | Axis labels and numbers are readable at the slide size. Only the series the message needs. Labels on the lines, or a short legend. The figure shows the message of the title. | Small text, many series, a method plot (PCA, QC) where the decision needs a result plot. |
| words | The words of the reader. Each abbreviation is explained or removed. Numbers are rounded to what matters (HR 1.3, not 1.2734). | Jargon, column names, code names, too many digits. |
| layout | Nothing is cut, overlaps, or runs off the slide. Elements line up. | Text is cut or overlaps. |
| decision | (Decision slide only.) Options, the recommended option, cost of error, and next steps with an owner are visible at once. | The reader cannot say what to do next. |

## Deck-level checks

- The deck title and slide 2 give the same answer as the brief.
- The story order: question → answer → evidence → limits → decision.
- The deck can be presented in 10 minutes: 4 to 6 slides plus the title slide.
